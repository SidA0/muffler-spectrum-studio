MODEL NOTES

What the curves mean

Helmholtz frequency

f₀ = c/(2π) √[Aₙ/(V Lₑ)]

Cavity volume, neck area, and effective neck length set the center frequency. The loss-Q control changes the estimated depth and width. Installed tuning changes with hot gas and flow.

Packed cone response

S(x) = πD(x)²/4   Z(x) = ρc/S(x)

A perforated cone has no single resonance equation. The site uses your free-muffler versus straight-pipe exports for the high-frequency baseline, then applies a small geometry and coupling modifier.

What to test next

Compare output spectra and dBC at the same RPM, load, meter position, and settings. Check pressure before the muffler: a sealed, flush side branch avoids a deliberate steady-flow restriction, but its junction and reflected pulses can affect the engine.

Show the loss equations and assumptions

Aₙ = πdₙ²/4, V = πDᵢ²Lᵥ/4, Lₑ = Lₙ + 0.85dₙ. The site subtracts a simplified side-branch transmission loss: TL = 10 log₁₀{[(R + Z₀/2)² + X²]/[R² + X²]}, where R = ω₀(Lₑ/Aₙ)/Q, X = ωLₑ/Aₙ − c²/(ωV), Z₀ = c/Aₚ, and ω = 2πf. Each branch is capped at 24 dB in the display.

For the packed section, the free-muffler versus straight-pipe octave differences set a frequency-dependent baseline. Packing depth, perforation open area and hole size scale that baseline. The cone contribution is an additional bounded trend centered on fᶜ = c/(2πLcone). These scaling terms are design heuristics; they have not been fitted to measured cone modifications.
