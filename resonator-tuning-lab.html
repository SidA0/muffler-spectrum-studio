MODEL

Math behind the curves

Quarter-wave tube

f₁ ≈ c / (4 Lq),   Zq = Rq − j(c/Aq) cot(2πfLq/c)
Rq = (c/Aq)/Qq

Helmholtz neck and cavity

V = πD_c²L_c/4;   Aₙ = πdₙ²/4;   Lₑ = Lₙ + 0.85dₙ
f₀ = c/(2π) √[Aₙ/(V Lₑ)]
Zh = Rh + j[2πfLₑ/Aₙ − c²/(2πfV)]
Rh = (2πf₀Lₑ/Aₙ)/Qh

From branch impedance to loss

TL = 20 log₁₀ |1 + Zmain/(2 Zbranch)|

Here c is sound speed in m/s, areas are m², lengths are m, volume is m³, j is the imaginary unit, and the displayed Q sliders set assumed acoustic resistance. Density cancels from these normalized impedance ratios.

For separate branches, the site multiplies two shunt transfer matrices with a length of main pipe between them. For a shared chamber, it transfers the neck impedance into a uniform closed cavity. The two layouts do not have the same modes.

  • A single Helmholtz cavity has one main low-frequency tuning; it does not cover 20–50 Hz by itself.
  • Small necks can have high oscillatory velocity and nonlinear loss in an exhaust. Peak height is especially uncertain.
  • At 20–50 Hz, the supplied Audacity exports have 46.875 Hz bins and cannot resolve the target peaks.
